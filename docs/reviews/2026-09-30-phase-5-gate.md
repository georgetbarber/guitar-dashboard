# Phase 5 evidence and real-input gate — 30 September 2026

## Status

The Phase 5 software path is implemented locally through packages 5A–5G. The assessment gate is **open**. George confirmed on 30 September that neither his guitar nor Pixel was available for a hands-on trial. Browser microphone input and learner actions in the tests are simulated; they do not establish guitar-input accuracy or whether the teaching route helps a person improve.

## What the app can claim now

- A self-reported attempt, a written self-review, and a checked on-screen answer are separate records. A correct theory answer cannot complete a playing task. An assisted or self-reported success cannot become verified physical mastery.
- Starting samples report pulse and sound control as learner reports; ear, fretboard and chord items check narrow answers. Unanswered areas stay unknown. The learner can override the suggested starting point.
- The pilot has an authored repair, a return to the whole phrase, a variation and a later-day self-reported check. Concept checks use a changed example on a later local day.
- The microphone offers three **temporary pitch-only signal estimates**: one sustained open E, two separate E plucks, and open E–G–E with quiet gaps. Missing or extra attacks, clipping, weak/unclear input, unstable pitch and delayed capture can return uncertain. No waveform, recording, result or measured-pitch evidence is retained or uploaded. These checks never advance the course.
- A learner can write one observation and one change after a temporary recording, reopen that text, and route the change into a smaller practice task and back to the phrase. The app does not analyse tone, muting, tension, phrasing or intention from that recording.

## What remains before the Phase 5 gate can close

1. **Guitar and installed-phone signal trial.** On the actual laptop and Pixel, in a quiet room and with ordinary background noise, try the open high E, the adjacent or deliberately wrong string, silence, a weak note and an overloaded input. Then try two E plucks with and without a true silence, and E–G–E on the high string at frets 0–3–0, including one wrong middle fret and an extra pluck. Check the displayed order against what was played, the uncertain paths, microphone release on stop/collapse/navigation, and whether latency or automatic gain on real hardware changes the thresholds. Revise the detector and wording from these observations before any measured-pitch record is introduced.
2. **One real review-to-retry journey.** Make a temporary take, compare it with the reference, write what was intended, what was heard or felt, and one change. Reopen it in Strengthen, try the suggested smaller exercise and return to both bars. Record whether the chosen exercise addresses the actual obstacle and whether the next take improves the intended detail. Test a muting/release review and a different focus; do not treat a learner's words as an automatic score.
3. **Delayed and changed-context learning.** Without live coaching, revisit the pilot on a later day and answer a changed concept example. Observe whether the learner remembers and transfers the relationship, not merely whether the app permits the clicks. A short personal trial on two or three initially uncertain capabilities can begin here; it continues into Phase 7.
4. **Evidence decision after the real-input trial.** If sustained or segmented pitch estimates prove reliable enough to retain, add a versioned `measured-pitch` record that names the exact signal task, confidence/uncertainty and actual conditions. Test its validation, backup, mixed-version sync and separation from self-reported playing, exact-answer knowledge and teacher-observed evidence. If reliability is insufficient, keep the checks temporary or remove the weaker ones; do not add measured mastery to satisfy a checklist.

Phase 6 content authoring should use what these trials teach about the first episode. The full 48-unit curriculum and Phase 7 release gate are separate work; their completion cannot be inferred from Phase 5 browser tests.
